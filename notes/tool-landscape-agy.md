# Feature Comparison: SATURN and Closed Strategic Transport Models vs. Open Tools for Motor Traffic Modelling

*Research note prepared for [`itsleeds/expansivemodelling`](https://github.com/itsleeds/expansivemodelling) — evaluating proprietary highway assignment suites against open-source ecosystems for car and heavy goods vehicle (HGV) traffic modelling.*

## 2. Closed Tools: Overview, Licensing Model, and Core Strengths

### 2.1 SATURN (AtkinsRéalis)
* **What it is:** **SATURN** (*Simulation and Assignment of Traffic to Urban Road Networks*) is a combined highway traffic simulation and assignment model originally developed at the Institute for Transport Studies (ITS), University of Leeds (introduced by Hall, Van Vliet & Willumsen in 1980 and Van Vliet in 1982) and commercially maintained and distributed by **AtkinsRéalis**. It combines macroscopic network assignment (`SATASS`) with a detailed mesoscopic junction simulation (`SATSIM`) in an iterative loop (`SATALL`).
* **Licence model:** Commercial proprietary licence distributed by AtkinsRéalis, typically sold as annual single-user (USB hardware dongle or soft-key) or multi-seat network licences. Pricing is tiered by **licence bands** (array dimension levels, up to large strategic tiers such as N2 and X7) that cap the maximum number of simulation nodes, buffer nodes, zones, and user classes. UK academic licences are available at reduced rates.
* **Where it excels:** SATURN is the de facto standard for UK local authority, regional, and National Highways scheme appraisal under **DfT Transport Analysis Guidance (TAG Unit M3.1)**. It uniquely excels at congested urban and peri-urban highway networks where delay is dominated by junction interactions (signals, roundabouts, priority give-ways), signal coordination (cyclic flow profiles), physical queue **blocking back**, and upstream **flow metering**, while seamlessly embedding those junctions inside a wider macroscopic **buffer network** and interfacing natively with **DIADEM** for variable demand modelling.

### 2.2 PTV Visum (PTV Group / Umovity)
* **What it is:** A comprehensive macroscopic and mesoscopic multimodal transport planning and travel demand modelling suite developed by PTV Group (now part of Umovity).
* **Licence model:** Commercial subscription or perpetual licence with annual maintenance, tiered by maximum network size (zones, links, nodes), optional add-on modules (e.g., Simulation-Based Dynamic Assignment, `TFlowFuzzy` matrix estimation, Activity-Based Modelling), and floating/named-user seats. Academic campus licences are available.
* **Where it excels:** Large-scale multimodal strategic models integrating highway assignment (including fast path-based LUCE and Tapas-like equilibrium solvers, Intersection Capacity Analysis [ICA], and Simulation-Based Dynamic Assignment [SBA]) with schedule- and frequency-based public transport assignment, Python/COM automation, and seamless sub-network export to **PTV Vissim** for microscopic simulation.

### 2.3 PTV Vistro (PTV Group / Umovity)
* **What it is:** An operational traffic engineering, intersection capacity analysis, signal timing optimisation, and local Traffic Impact Analysis (TIA) tool.
* **Licence model:** Commercial per-seat annual subscription (significantly lower cost than full regional suites like Visum).
* **Where it excels:** Rapid site-impact studies, development trip generation/distribution, corridor signal coordination optimization, and deterministic intersection Level of Service (LoS) evaluation (HCM, Kimber roundabouts) with one-click export to PTV Vissim. Unlike Visum or SATURN, it is not a regional iterative equilibrium assignment model.

### 2.4 Bentley OpenPaths EMME (formerly INRO EMME)
* **What it is:** A high-performance macroscopic multimodal transport planning system originally created at the University of Montreal / INRO and acquired by Bentley Systems under the **OpenPaths** brand.
* **Licence model:** Commercial enterprise subscription managed via Bentley's licensing portal (and OpenPaths tiers), historically scaled by zone limits and parallel core counts.
* **Where it excels:** Mathematical rigor, convergence stability, and speed on massive metropolitan networks (e.g., TfL's London models, North American MPOs). Its Origin-Based User Equilibrium (SOLA / EMMESOL) highway assignment and transit assignment algorithms achieve machine-precision relative gaps ($10^{-6}$ or better), supported by a clean Python API (`inro.emme.desktop` / `Modeller`) and tight coupling with **Dynameq** for mesoscopic DTA.

### 2.5 Caliper TransCAD
* **What it is:** A fully integrated Geographic Information System (GIS) and travel demand modelling platform developed by Caliper Corporation.
* **Licence model:** Commercial per-seat or network licence (standard, professional, and enterprise tiers) bundled with Caliper's proprietary GIS engine, with academic pricing available.
* **Where it excels:** North American DOT statewide and MPO regional models where native GIS topology editing, spatial data conflation, multi-class highway assignment (including Origin-UE and BFW), freight/HGV modelling, and GISDK/Python scripting in a single desktop application eliminate external GIS preprocessing. Coupled with **TransModeler** for hybrid meso/micro simulation.

### 2.6 Bentley OpenPaths CUBE (formerly Citilabs CUBE)
* **What it is:** A modular macroscopic demand modelling suite (**CUBE Voyager**), matrix estimation tool (**CUBE Analyst** / **Analyst Drive**), and mesoscopic DTA package (**CUBE Avenue**), now consolidated into Bentley OpenPaths.
* **Licence model:** Commercial subscription under Bentley OpenPaths, licensed by module and compute cluster/thread capacity.
* **Where it excels:** Highly customized four-step and activity-based regional planning frameworks driven by **Voyager Script** (matrix manipulation, highway/transit skimming, toll-road revenue forecasting) and cloud/cluster scenario execution. Widely used in the US, UK (e.g., regional multi-modal models), and Australasia.

### 2.7 OmniTRANS (DAT.Mobility / Goudappel — *OmniTRANS Powered by Bentley OpenPaths*)
* **What it is:** A multimodal strategic transport modelling suite developed by DAT.Mobility (part of Dutch consultancy Goudappel), now transitioning from *OmniTRANS Expert* (supported until 2030) to **OmniTRANS Powered by Bentley OpenPaths**.
* **Licence model:** Commercial tiered licensing (with a free demo capped at 25 zones) distributed across Europe by Goudappel/DAT.Mobility and Bentley.
* **Where it excels:** Integrated multi-modal modelling (car, HGV, public transport, and detailed cycling/active travel networks), built-in macroscopic dynamic assignment (**MaDAM**) with detailed junction delay calculation, population synthesis, and Python/Ruby workflow scripting—dominant in Dutch national, provincial, and municipal transport planning.

### 2.8 Aimsun Next & Simulation Alternatives (Aimsun, PTV Vissim, Bentley Dynameq, TransModeler)
* **What it is:** **Aimsun Next** is a commercial multi-resolution transport modelling environment embedding macroscopic static assignment, mesoscopic DTA, microscopic car-following/lane-changing simulation, and hybrid meso-micro simulation inside a single application and data model.
* **Licence model:** Commercial annual subscription or perpetual licence tiered by edition (*Pro*, *Advanced*, *Expert*) and enabled resolutions (Macro, Meso, Micro, Travel Demand Modelling, and HPC thread packs).
* **Where it excels:** Corridor and sub-regional operational schemes (smart motorways, bus priority, active traffic management, emissions modelling) where static assignment is too coarse and modellers need dynamic user equilibrium (DUE), time-dependent OD matrix adjustment, and seamless zooming from macroscopic demand skims to mesoscopic queues and microscopic junction physics without lossy file conversion between separate tools.

## 3. What Open Tools Are Missing to Fully Replace SATURN-Type Workflows

Open-source tools have already surpassed SATURN and legacy commercial suites in several areas crucial to modern data science—most notably **automated OpenStreetMap network cleaning and conflation** (`osmget`, `osmactive`, `netclean`, `netweld`), **zone-free building/POI-level routing** (`netassign`), **harmonised multi-sensor count ingestion** (`countsget`), and **containerised, out-of-sample cross-validated benchmarking** (`aadtbench`, `csis`). For example, on the `aadtbench` `leeds-v1` benchmark (417 car count sites, 415 heavy count sites), uncalibrated **AequilibraE** (`ue_bfw` and `aon`), **`cityseer`**, and **`sDNA`** achieve Spearman rank correlations of $\rho \approx 0.71\text{–}0.73$ for car and $\rho \approx 0.64\text{–}0.69$ for heavy traffic out of the box in under a few minutes in Docker.

However, to **fully replace a SATURN highway assignment workflow** for formal scheme appraisal (such as UK DfT TAG Unit M3.1 business cases, junction redesigns, or road-space reallocation with congestion spillback), open tools currently lack six key capabilities:

1. **Co-Iterative Junction Simulation & Assignment (Turn-Specific Delay Curves):**
   * *The gap:* In SATURN, `SATSIM` simulates each junction's signal timings, staging, intergreens, priority give-way gap acceptance, and roundabout entry/circulating capacities to fit turn-specific flow-delay curves that feed directly back into `SATASS` route choice at every iteration.
   * *Current open state:* **AequilibraE** explicitly lacks turn penalties and turn bans in its path-building/assignment Cython core (congestion is strictly link-based via BPR). **`netassign`** supports static turn penalties (`left_m`, `right_m`, `cross_m`), OSM turn restrictions (`penalty_s`), and fixed signal penalties (`signals_m`) on an edge-based graph, and **`csis/py/congest.py`** runs iterative link-based BPR congestion (`msa`, `fw`, `inc`), but neither computes flow-dependent opposing-turn delays (e.g., right-turn delay as a function of oncoming straight flow) or roundabout/signal capacity saturation inside the assignment loop.
2. **Cyclic Flow Profiles (Peloton Dispersion) and Signal Coordination:**
   * *The gap:* SATURN models traffic at simulation nodes using **cyclic flow profiles (CFPs)** (inspired by TRANSYT), capturing how platoons released by an upstream traffic signal arrive at a downstream signal depending on link travel time, platoon dispersion, and signal offset.
   * *Current open state:* Open static assignment tools (`AequilibraE`, `netassign`, `Quetzal`) treat flow as a uniform rate over the modelled period. Capturing signal offset effects in open tools currently requires dropping all the way down to full microscopic simulation in **SUMO**, which is much heavier to calibrate at city scale.
3. **Physical Queue Spillback ("Blocking Back") and Upstream Flow Metering:**
   * *The gap:* In congested urban networks, when a link's demand exceeds downstream junction capacity ($V/C > 1$), SATURN's quasi-dynamic `PassQ` and `SATSIM` mechanics track physical queue length, **block back** across upstream junctions to penalise cross-movements, and **meter** (throttle) flow reaching downstream bottlenecks. Standard BPR link functions allow $V/C > 1$ to pass downstream unrealistically.
   * *Current open state:* Static BPR in `AequilibraE` and `csis/py/congest.py` allows overloaded links to discharge their full volume downstream. Only dynamic queue/micro simulators (**MATSim** `QSim`, **SUMO**, **UXsim**) enforce physical storage capacity and spillback, and bridging clean static assignment models with light-weight quasi-dynamic queue metering remains an open research gap.
4. **Native Entropy/Bi-Level OD Matrix Estimation (SATME2 Equivalent) with Guardrails:**
   * *The gap:* Consultancy workflows rely heavily on `SATPIJA` + `SATME2` (Maximum Entropy Matrix Estimation) to update prior OD matrices against directional link and turning counts while monitoring UK DfT TAG matrix-distortion diagnostics (trip length distribution shifts, regression slopes/$R^2$ between prior and posterior cells).
   * *Current open state:* `AequilibraE` provides IPF/Furness (`ipf`) and synthetic gravity models (`SyntheticGravityModel`) but lacks a packaged path-proportion (`PIJA`) + count-constrained entropy/GLS matrix estimation module. Meanwhile, `netfit` in `csis` takes a statistically sounder regression approach—fitting leg weights and elasticities to counts with out-of-sample LODO validation and NTS `km_per_person` constraints—which avoids `SATME2`'s notorious overfitting, but cannot output a TAG-compliant "posterior OD matrix" matched to individual turning counts.
5. **Automated OSM-to-Junction-Coding Extraction (Lanes, Flares, Signal Phases):**
   * *The gap:* Even if an open assignment engine supported SATURN-style turn-capacity formulas (HCM / Kimber roundabouts / PICADY priority / Webster-Cobbe signals), those formulas require per-arm flare lengths, turning lane allocations, give-way conflict matrices, and signal stage timings.
   * *Current open state:* `osmget`, `osmactive`, `netclean`, `openroadsget`, and `junctionareas` already automate highway topology cleaning, roundabout ring collapsing, arm counting, and kerb corner radius fitting from OSM and OS MasterMap. However, inferring turn-lane channelisation (`turn:lanes`), flare storage, and realistic default signal timings from OSM tags into an edge-based junction capacity solver is not yet implemented.
6. **Turnkey UK DfT TAG M3.1 Validation & Variable Demand (DIADEM) Reporting:**
   * *The gap:* SATURN workflows produce standardized TAG Unit M3.1 tables out of the box (screenline $\pm 5\%$ checks, link/turn GEH $< 5$ for $>85\%$ of counts, journey time route validation within $\pm 15\%$ or 1 minute) and couple directly with **DIADEM** for hierarchical logit variable demand (frequency, mode, destination, time-of-day shifts) and **TUBA** for economic appraisal.
   * *Current open state:* `aadtbench` and `csis` already have superior statistical validation (blind spatial group-out folds, leave-one-city-out transferability, Spearman $\rho$, $\log R^2$), and adding a standard TAG M3.1 screenline/GEH report + open cost-damping VDM loop on top of `AequilibraE` or `netassign` is straightforward in Python/Rust, but has not yet been packaged as a drop-in module.
